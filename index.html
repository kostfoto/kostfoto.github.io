About Me

My name is Konstantinos Fotopoulos, and I am a soon to be Computer Science MSc student at ETH Zurich. After the Master's degree at ETH, I hope to continue my academic journey by pursuing a PhD.

In 2024, I earned my Diploma (an integrated BSc and MEng degree) in Electrical and Computer Engineering from the National Technical University of Athens. For my thesis, supervised by Prof. Petros Maragos, I explored the use of tropical geometry in neural network compression.

After finishing my degreee, I worked as a research collaborator at the Athena Research Center, working again under the supervision of Prof. Maragos. My research focused on the intersection of machine learning, tropical geometry, and mathematical morphology. I investigated the challenges of training morphological neural networks, and worked torwards trainable morholoigcal neural network architectures.

Research Experience

  • Research Collaborator Dec. 2024* – June 2025
    Robotics Institute, Athena Research Center
    Under the supervision of Prof. Petros Maragos, I conducted research in the area of morphological neural networks. Although my official employment began in February 2025, I started contributing to the project in December 2024, when I turned in my first report. In collaboration with a PhD student, I explored the application of morphological networks to acoustic data and proposed the Sparse-Morph architecture, which demonstrated modest improvements in pruning efficiency and convergence speed—work that led to a paper accepted at EUSIPCO 2025. In parallel, I pursued research on the training of deep morphological networks. I formally showed that, despite their inherent non-linearity, these networks benefit from "linear" activations. I designed four architectures under three different constraint settings and proved that two of them are universal approximators. Empirical results confirmed that my network are trainable, one of my networks significantly accelerated gradient descent when trained with large batches, and my proposed architectures showed better pruning characteristics than their linear counterparts. These findings are currently under review at NeurIPS.
  • MEng Thesis Student Nov. 2023 – Oct. 2024
    School of Electrical and Computer Engineering, NTUA
    Under the supervision of Prof. Petros Maragos, I conducted research on the intersection of Tropical Geometry and Machine Learning. My work led to the development of a refined theorem for the approximation of tropical polynomials, along with a novel algorithm for neural network compression. My method improved upon a prior algorithm of another student. I provided theoretical guarantees for my algorithm’s performance and performed comparative experiments. My algorithm demonstrated competitive results with strong methods such as ThiNet and CUP—marking the first time a tropical geometric approach reached this level of performance.
